How these ratings think

Probability here isn’t a fixed number read off long-run averages. It’s a measure of current confidence, and it changes as evidence arrives.

Start with an informed opinion. Before a season, each team’s rating is a prior built from last season and from the players on this year’s roster, each carrying his own rating to wherever he plays now.

Let the games speak. Every result moves the rating, and the prior’s share of the weight shrinks as games accumulate. Each team page draws both beliefs as curves: the wide preseason opinion and the narrower current one.

Test everything on games the model hasn’t seen. Every choice, from how much last season counts to whether distance matters in March, was decided by replaying past seasons and predicting games before they happened. What didn’t help was left out, and the Method pages say so.

Without data, all we have is opinion; without context, all we have is data. The goal is to bring the two together.
